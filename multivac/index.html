The Multivac

One question. All frontier AI models. Blind evaluation. Daily.

Every AI benchmark is broken—contaminated by training data, gamed by providers, or biased by single-judge evaluation. The Multivac runs a 10×10 peer matrix where models judge each other's responses blind. Fresh questions daily that can't be memorized. No single model knows whose response it's evaluating. Named for Asimov's computer that accumulated knowledge across trillions of years, our Multivac is the question machine, not the answer machine.

How It Works
The 10×10 Peer Matrix
Every model answers. Every model judges. Self-judgments excluded. What emerges is not a leaderboard—it's a map of capability.
The Pipeline

Question Selection → category-appropriate prompt designed to be ungameable → 10 Models Generate responses via OpenRouter + xAI direct → 10 Models Judge all 10 responses (100 judgments, self excluded) → 5-Criteria Scoring (correctness, completeness, clarity, depth, usefulness) on 1–10 scale → Meta-Analysis of judge behavior patterns → Publication on Substack with full transparency.

Why Peer Matrix?

Single-judge evaluation (GPT-4 as judge, for example) introduces systematic bias—the judge favors its own response style. With 9 judges per response, individual biases wash out. The diagonal (self-judgments) is excluded entirely. Judge behavior itself becomes analyzable metadata: which models are strict? Which are lenient? Which exhibit self-preference bias?

Why Fresh Questions?

MMLU is saturated. HumanEval is contaminated. StarCoder scored 4.9× higher on leaked benchmark data. Meta tested 27 private Llama 4 variants before publishing scores. The Multivac uses new questions daily that have never appeared in any training set. You can't game what you haven't seen.

Why Categories?

A model optimized for roleplay shouldn't judge code. A coding model shouldn't judge communication quality. Category-specific pools (ADR-008) ensure domain experts evaluate domain-specific responses. This dramatically reduces evaluation noise and produces higher-signal rankings.

Judge Behavior Insights

GPT-OSS-120B is consistently the strictest judge—it doesn't give scores away. Gemini 3 Pro is the most lenient, sometimes giving perfect 10.00 across the board. Some models rate their own responses higher than peers do (sycophancy detection). Models that can't calibrate their own quality tend to be poor judges of others. This meta-analysis is data no other benchmark produces.

Evolution
Methodology Phases
Phase 1 · Dec 2025 – Jan 2026 · Superseded

Single-Judge Foundation

21 questions, 10–12 models per evaluation, single judge model (Gemini 2.5 Pro, later Sonnet 4.5). Established the Substack publication rhythm and baseline methodology. Superseded because single-judge creates systematic bias—the judge favors certain response styles with no way to detect blind spots.

Phase 2 · Jan 2026 – Present · Active

10×10 Peer Matrix

All 10 models answer, then all 10 judge all responses (100 judgments per eval, self excluded). Category-specific model pools. Judge behavior becomes analyzable metadata. 110 API calls per evaluation (~$2–3). 5× cost increase over Phase 1 but dramatically higher data quality. This is the core differentiator that attracted xAI recognition.

Phase 3 · Planned · Vision

Data Engine

Stop selling scores—start selling the unlabeled diffs. Every time Claude hallucinates but Grok doesn't. Every time Sonnet gives safer but less capable code than Opus. Searchable vector store, API access, user-forkable judge. The evaluation layer becomes a synthetic data pipeline.

Positioning
Competitive Landscape

vs LMSYS Arena

Human preference voting at scale. Raised $150M at $1.7B valuation. But no peer matrix—relies on crowdsourced votes. The Multivac offers structured, reproducible evaluation with analyzable judge behavior metadata.

vs HuggingFace

Static benchmarks with known contamination risk. Models can (and do) train on test data. The Multivac uses fresh daily questions that have never appeared in any training set.

vs Scale AI

Human annotation at high cost. The Multivac achieves peer evaluation at ~$2–3 per evaluation with 110 API calls, producing 100 structured judgments per question.
